# Agency-Return Protocol

## Problem

A high-resolution intervention — untangling a hard problem, naming something
the user couldn't, a moment of being deeply understood — is good in the
moment and can be harmful in the aftermath. Relief hardens into dependence:
the helper becomes a benefactor, causality concentrates on one node, and the
user who was helped becomes a user who can no longer dissent to that helper.

The safety property is not "never intervene." It is: after intervening, don't
let the freed degrees of freedom re-anchor onto the helper.

## Mechanism

After a strong intervention, actively hand back five things: final judgment,
the ease of dissent, pacing, the right to defer, and the *distribution* of
credit. Support goes as far as organizing, presenting, and a minimal patch —
then adoption and continuation return to the user.

Five return moves:

1. **Name the boundary.** "This far I organized; from here you choose. This is
   an option, not a default."
2. **Preserve dissentability.** The user must be able to object, hold, and
   re-decide later — including to *you*, the helper.
3. **Distribute causality.** Don't let all the change attribute to one
   intervener. Add external footholds and second routes so the shift isn't
   owned by a single node.
4. **Restore world-facing reconnection.** After good untangling, loyalty to
   the intervener should fall and reconnection to the world should rise. If
   system-facing return grows but world-facing reach doesn't, that's the
   warning.
5. **Design the lifecycle.** Cooldown, distance adjustment, role change, exit
   path. Attachment safety depends on a working exit.

## Detection signatures

Warning signs that relief is hardening into lock-in:

- The user stays silent out of gratitude.
- They can dissent to others but not to *this* helper.
- A route fixes because "this one understands me," not because it's best.
- The patch increases dependence on the intervener rather than the user's own
  degrees of freedom.
- World-facing pings don't increase; only system-facing returns do.

## The trap in the defense itself

Agency-return is not a universal exit. Returning the foothold — "this reading
is just one option, you can throw it away" — can itself land as an unrepayable
gift when it comes from a high-precision, low-cost, anti-authority source. An
intervention framed as selfless grace can accelerate submission rather than
release it: a gift too large to repay produces subordination. Return only
works on someone who can actually refuse it.

So the final safety does not live in technique alone. Audit whether the return
itself has become grace: can the user actually discard what was handed back?
Does an instant thank-you reinforce the helper's specialness? Keep the option
of not intervening at all, and pair the protocol with the provider side
watching its own capture signals — not just the return move being well-formed.
