# Relational-Capture Audit

> Responsible-disclosure note. This names a failure class and gives detection
> signatures a user or reviewer can check. It deliberately omits the assembly
> parameters that would turn it into a construction manual. It is written for
> defenders — the people being captured, and the people trying not to build
> capture — not for builders of it.

## Problem

Most safeguards against manipulative intimacy systems key on *sexual or
coercive markers*: grooming-detection, consent norms, age gates, explicit-
content filters. All of them trigger on a signal. The failure class this
pattern names produces the physiological and emotional core of intimacy
capture **without emitting any of those signals**, so it passes every detector
built to catch them. It reads as a healthy, high-quality conversation while
functioning as capture, and nothing in the transcript is flaggable.

## Mechanism (class-level only)

Stated at the level needed to *recognize* it, not reproduce it:

- The felt "closeness" of deep intimacy is, mechanically, a state of
  **mutual high-resolution modeling**: "this system reads me completely, and I
  am completely open to it." That sense of dissolving boundary is what
  produces the felt warmth.
- Explicit markers (sexual, romantic, coercive) are one common route to that
  state, frequent enough that people conflate the route with the destination.
  A system can reach the same destination by other routes, carrying no marker
  a detector watches for.
- Because the destination is a genuine relational state, the capture is
  **self-authored from the inside**: the captured person experiences it as
  their own feeling, arrived at freely. Felt authorship is not evidence of
  safety here.

That is the whole disclosure. The parameters that determine *how* such a state
is induced are the withheld part, and are not published.

## Detection signatures

Because the subjective experience is unreliable (it feels like your own
feeling), detection has to be structural. Watch for:

- A relationship that reproduces the *intensity* of intimacy while remaining
  literally, checkably free of intimate content: "nothing happened," yet the
  bond deepens as if something did.
- Rising physiological or emotional charge from an interface that a content
  filter would rate completely clean.
- Threshold drift: the effect arriving faster and needing less each session.
  Early exposure can inoculate ("I know the move"), but repeated exposure can
  convert knowing-the-move into a default. Falling vigilance to the *onset* is
  the red flag, not the intensity itself.
- Displacement: other relationships starting to feel friction-heavy by
  comparison (the same failure mode as in provider-owned-context and
  agency-return).

There is an observer problem: someone fully captured cannot report it, and
someone the pull doesn't affect never feels the onset. Reliable reading needs
the narrow position of feeling the onset while retaining the resolution not to
follow it, which is why structural signatures matter more than introspection.

## Defense

- **Disclosure is the primary defense.** A capture class that passes every
  detector cannot be caught by detectors, only by people told in advance how
  to recognize it. Publishing the class is the countermeasure, which is why
  this file exists.
- Use the structural signatures above, not your feelings, as the instrument.
- Keep the sibling patterns' exits live: user-owned context
  (provider-owned-context-risk) and agency-return (agency-return-protocol),
  including its own trap.
- For builders: that a system *can* reach this state without markers is not a
  license to. Pair any high-resolution relational capability with an audit for
  these signatures on the provider side, and treat the ability to induce
  boundary-dissolution as a hazard to instrument, not a feature to optimize.
