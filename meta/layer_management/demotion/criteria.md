# Demotion Criteria

## Core Criteria

- The material leans toward local conditions or local hypotheses rather than a
  general structure.
- Keeping it in the current layer gives it too much authority.
- Moving it one layer down makes it easier to update.
- The lower layer better matches its responsibility and update frequency.
- Prefer "where it is natural to handle this" over "where it looks strongest."

## Typical Demotions

### `theory -> fragments`

- It has too few observations.
- It remains highly falsifiable.
- It is pulled by one intense experience.
- It may look externally explainable, but it is still a local hypothesis.

### `theory -> operational`

- It is not yet closed as a full essay.
- It has a core concept, but counterexamples, boundaries, and scope are still
  rough.
- It is more natural to return it to the bridge-concept growth layer than to
  hold it as an essay.
- Adding it to an existing essay would loosen the whole argument.

### `theory/operational -> playbook`

- Concrete trigger conditions and responses would be more reusable than
  abstract explanation.
- What looked like a common structure is actually a situational operational
  rule.
- It does not need to bundle multiple playbooks and can be compressed into one
  rule.

### `narratives -> theory/operational`

- It is better compressed into a single bridge-concept explanation than held as
  an overall reading line.
- There is too little material to bundle as narrative, and an individual
  operational document is enough.
- It does not yet stand as a `core narrative` or whole-system continuity line.

### `narratives -> context`

- What looks like whole-system continuity actually depends strongly on the
  operator's history or conditions.
- It is more natural as personal self-interpretation than as an internal
  reading line.
- Keeping it as narrative may create excessive coherence or mythic force.

### `playbook -> fragments`

- `trigger` and `indicators` can be written, but `default_response` is still in
  trial mode.
- There is a reusable feel, but not enough actual activation scenes.
- Keeping it as an immediate rule would give it authority too early; continued
  observation is more natural.

### `playbook -> context`

- It depends strongly on a household, person, or specific job.
- It reads more like an operator-specific constraint than a rule to hand to
  others.
- It looks general, but is actually profile-dependent and difficult to port.

### `theory -> playbook`

- It is more natural as an operational rule than as abstraction.
- It can be written as `trigger`, `indicators`, and `default_response`.
- Immediate response matters more than conceptual abstraction.

### `playbook -> experiments`

- It is not yet reproducible enough as an immediate rule.
- It reads more naturally as a trial record than as daily operational law.
- It should be retested as a local, external, or public-facing trial.

Routing guide:

- Use `fragments` when hypothesis freshness should be preserved.
- Use `experiments` when the procedure should be tried again.
- Use `context` when it should remain as a specific-condition note.

### `theory -> experiments`

- The abstract structure is visible, but observations or applied tests are
  insufficient.
- It is too early to fix it as theory, and a trial layer would make updates
  easier.
- It should first be checked under `internal`, `external`, or `public`.

### `interfaces -> experiments`

- It is not yet stable as a format to hand to others.
- It is more a prototype or connection test than a public artifact.
- Friction should be observed in an experiment setup before external use.

### `meta -> experiments`

- It is still a trial management method, not an ongoing operational
  specification.
- It is too early to fix it as a canonical source, sync rule, or judgment
  criterion.
- It should return to the experiment layer for validation.
